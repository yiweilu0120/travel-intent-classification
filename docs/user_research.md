## 1. Research Background

The existing STP logic classifies "Leisure Travel" and "Necessary-purpose" primarily based on objective indicators such as travel distance, destination type, and check-in date, using 50 km as the boundary between local and inter-city travel, with local scenarios almost always defaulting to "Necessary-purpose". As the business application deepened, this logic revealed clear misalignment issues:

- **Core Problem**: Some local leisure travel demand is incorrectly classified as necessary-purpose scenarios, resulting in insufficient precision in travel scenario identification and affecting the business's ability to capture opportunities.
- **Research Objective**: From the user's perspective, refine the definition of leisure travel scenarios, and understand under what circumstances users consider themselves to be "traveling" and where the boundary between "leisure travel" and "non-travel" lies.

## 2. Research Methodology

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| **Research Method** | One-on-one qualitative interviews, exploring users' subjective perception and boundary judgment of "leisure travel" |
| **Sample Size** | 50 respondents |
| **Research Period** | April 2026 |

## 3. Core Conclusions

Users' perception of "leisure travel" is **not determined by distance or geographic boundaries**, but is jointly defined by three dimensions: **psychological state, actual experience, and social companionship**:

> The mindset has switched to relaxation mode, something new has been experienced, and time is spent with people who matter — then it counts as leisure travel.

Actual scenarios therefore extend far beyond traditional inter-city trips, covering the following ten categories:

1. Long-distance family / parent-child trips
2. Couple / partner city trips
3. Long-distance trips with friends
4. Weekend / short holiday nearby short trips
5. Local / city leisure activities
6. Anniversary / special event driven travel
7. Visiting hometown / relatives with leisure activities
8. Group family trips with friends
9. Long-stay travel / slow travel
10. Business trips with leisure activities

## 4. Dimensions Defining Leisure Travel

Users do not have a unified standard for defining "leisure travel", but it can be summarized into three core dimensions:

### 1. Psychological State

The vast majority of users define leisure travel as **relaxation, stress relief, and a change of environment**, emphasizing that the essence of leisure travel is a pure leisure activity away from daily work or high-pressure life.

### 2. Experience and Exploration

Some users emphasize the fresh experiences and broadened horizons gained during leisure travel, defining leisure travel as experiencing local customs and culture, viewing cultural landscapes, connecting with nature, checking in at landmarks, etc.

### 3. Social and Emotional

Still other users place the emphasis on companions when defining leisure travel, believing that the core of leisure travel is spending time with specific people, such as parent-child companionship, couple dates, etc.

### Elements Constituting Leisure Travel

Users' judgment on "what constitutes a trip of leisure travel" can be understood from two dimensions: **necessary factors** (without which it does not count as leisure travel) and **sufficient factors** (with which it counts as leisure travel):

| Factor Type | Most Mentioned Elements | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Necessary Factors** | Actual experience content (18 people) | Local cuisine, landmark check-ins, cultural experiences, natural scenery, etc. |
| | Accommodation experience (10 people) | Staying overnight away from home is an important marker of leisure travel |
| | Companions (8 people) | Having companions is a key element of leisure travel |
| | Emotional satisfaction (7 people) | Only when the mindset is right does it count as leisure travel |
| | Spatial movement (3 people) | Leaving the usual place of residence |
| **Sufficient Factors** | Actual experience content (20 people) | As long as it is experienced, it counts as leisure travel |
| | Spatial movement (12 people) | Arriving at a new place alone is enough to count as leisure travel |
| | Companions (9 people) | Being with the right people is enough to count as leisure travel |
| | Emotional satisfaction (7 people) | Having fun is enough to count as leisure travel |
| | Accommodation experience (6 people) | Staying at a hotel is enough to count as leisure travel |

**Key Insights**:

- **Actual experience content** is the most mentioned element among both necessary and sufficient factors; leisure travel ultimately comes down to "what was done and what was experienced".
- **Spatial movement** is the element with the largest contrast between necessary and sufficient: only 3 people regard it as necessary, while 12 regard it as sufficient. Most users would not deny a trip as leisure travel simply because they "did not leave the local area", yet "arriving at a new place" alone is enough to make people feel it counts as leisure travel.

## 5. Leisure Travel vs. Non-travel Boundary

### Judgment Criteria

1. **Whether the mindset has switched to a relaxed / enjoyment state**: whether the primary purpose at departure is leisure, whether there is ample autonomously controllable time during the trip, and whether the actual experience meets expectations.
2. **Whether the destination offers a sense of novelty**.
3. **Whether traveling with others**: especially for family users, the absence of family / partner companionship significantly weakens the sense of leisure travel.

### Scenarios Clearly Not Counting as Leisure Travel

- Accommodation for inter-city exams
- Pure business trips
- Hosting friends locally / staying out too late and not wanting to go home

### Gray Areas

- Staying an extra day on a business trip / business trips with leisure activities
- Visiting relatives with leisure activities
- Same-city / short-distance hotel stays
- Revisiting a place already visited / too familiar
- Plans being disrupted / experience falling short of expectations
- Meeting friends / partner with leisure activities

---

## 6. The Role of Accommodation in Leisure Travel

Overall, accommodation is one of the necessary factors for leisure travel — if there is no overnight hotel stay, the trip may not count as leisure travel.

Users' positioning on accommodation is that **meeting basic requirements is sufficient**:

- Ensuring cleanliness, hygiene, and comfort
- Some users are willing to pay a premium for experience upgrades such as scenic-view rooms
- Younger users may be interested in experience-oriented amenities such as bathtubs and audio-visual facilities

## 7. Leisure Travel Scenario Classification

### Scenario Distribution Overview

| Scenario | Share |
|---|---:|
| Weekend / Short Holiday Nearby Trips | 18.8% |
| Local / City Leisure Activities | 18.5% |
| Couple City Trips | 15.7% |
| Long-distance Trips with Friends | 15.2% |
| Long-distance Family / Parent-child Trips | 9.2% |
| Business Trips with Leisure Activities | 7.7% |
| Visiting Hometown / Relatives with Leisure Activities | 5.1% |
| Group Family Trips with Friends | 4.1% |
| Anniversary / Special Event Trips | 3.2% |
| Long-stay Travel / Slow Travel | 2.2% |

### 1. Long-distance Family / Parent-child Trips

- **Travel Unit**: Family (couple + children, or three generations traveling together)
- **Duration**: 5-8 days
- **Timing**: Highly concentrated on long holidays such as Spring Festival and National Day
- **Decision Behavior**: Planned 1-2 weeks or even earlier in advance; researches guides on Xiaohongshu, compares prices from multiple sources to book rooms
- **Travel Mode**: Self-driving or group tours
- **Destination Preference**: Cities with clear climate or cultural experience appeal

### 2. Couple / Partner City Trips

- **Travel Unit**: Two people as a pair
- **Duration**: 3-5 days
- **Style**: Mainly "strolling, eating, and taking photos" with a relaxed and casual pace
- **Destination Choice**: Influenced by seasonal events (Wuhan cherry blossom season), social media seeding (Yanji internet-famous wall), and cuisine appeal (Shenzhen, Chaoshan)
- **Decision Behavior**: Shorter and more flexible decision chains, sometimes "just discussing it and going"

### 3. Long-distance Trips with Friends

- **Travel Unit**: 2-5 friends as a group
- **Duration**: 4-7 days
- **Demographics**: Mostly young people or groups with relatively flexible schedules
- **Core Driver**: Social companionship is the foundation; the travel opportunity often arises when both parties' schedules happen to align

### 4. Weekend / Short Holiday Nearby Short Trips

- **Duration**: 1-2 days, returning the same day or the next day
- **Decision Behavior**: Lightweight decisions, spur-of-the-moment departures, almost no advance planning needed; the time from decision to departure may be only a few hours
- **Transportation**: High-speed rail and self-driving mainly
- **Accommodation**: Usually only one night's stay; some attention to the hotel experience but it is not the focus

### 5. Local / City Leisure Activities

- **Activities**: Visiting parks, viewing exhibitions, museums, camping, theme parks, hot springs, etc.
- **Duration**: Half a day to one day
- **Core Consensus**: "Leaving home counts as leisure travel"
- **Decision Behavior**: Casual, usually no complex planning required
- **Characteristics**: Parent-child scenarios focus on supporting facilities (fishing, bonfires, fireworks and other activities); some users choose local hot spring hotels, camping bases and other "micro-vacation" products

### 6. Anniversary / Special Event Driven Travel

- **Trigger Events**: Concerts, music festivals, anniversaries, etc.
- **Itinerary Characteristics**: Centered around the event, with leisure experiences of the destination's food, accommodation, and play
- **Destination Choice**: Determined by the event location, not a self-directed choice
- **Decision Behavior**: Advance actions taken (grabbing tickets / booking accommodation); in anniversary scenarios, hotels with a sense of ceremony are deliberately chosen

### 7. Visiting Hometown / Relatives with Leisure Activities

- **Primary Purpose**: Returning to hometown or visiting relatives
- **Characteristics**: On this basis, also visiting local or surrounding attractions and tasting cuisine
- **Definition Boundary**: Users themselves consider it "counting as leisure travel", but acknowledge it is not pure leisure travel
- **Decision Behavior**: Less guide preparation; more about "seeing what fun things are nearby upon arrival"

### 8. Group Family Trips with Friends

- **Travel Unit**: Two or more families (parents of children's classmates, or friend families with good relationships)
- **Characteristics**: Combines social and parent-child attributes
- **Transportation**: Self-driving is a common mode (multiple cars traveling together)
- **Itinerary**: Interleaves internet-famous check-in spots and in-depth experience projects

### 9. Long-stay Travel / Slow Travel

- **Duration**: Two weeks to a month or even longer
- **Mode**: Not a traditional "few-day tour", but a deep experience of "living in a different place"
- **Decision Behavior**: Longer advance planning cycles, slow itinerary pace, emphasis on in-depth experience rather than check-ins
- **Accommodation**: More cautious selection; some users choose to rent apartments in local quality residential communities; if choosing hotels, more attention is paid to "quality-to-price ratio"

### 10. Business Trips with Leisure Activities

- **Scenario**: Arriving in a city for work and using spare time to visit local attractions or taste local cuisine
- **Characteristics**: The trip itself is not for leisure, but users proactively turn the business trip into a mini travel experience
