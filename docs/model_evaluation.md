# STP Model Evaluation

## Conclusion

This round of research collected **1,811** valid order samples (interviews + surveys), of which **644** could be matched against the initial model's labeling results. **Interview match rate: 63.6%; survey match rate: 51.7%.** For the interview samples, a user-behavior analysis was conducted on the MT UA platform; after excluding noisy and boundary-ambiguous samples, **the interview accuracy improved from 63.6% to 72.4%**.

**1. Feature Optimization Directions**

**The `intention_prob` distributions are highly overlapping, so threshold tuning cannot resolve this — only feature optimization can.** The current model is generally biased toward Leisure Travel *(interview accuracy: 83% for Leisure Travel, only 20% for Necessary-purpose)*. Optimization should not only "add Leisure Travel signals" but must also **add Necessary-purpose features for counterbalance**; otherwise, when neither Leisure Travel nor Necessary-purpose signals are strong, the model still defaults to Leisure Travel. Based on clear misclassification cases, 3 priority feature optimization directions were identified:

- **① Incorporate ticketing / vacation session signal features**: 3 of the 5 clear Leisure Travel misclassifications involved a "Hotel & Travel BU - Domestic Vacation BU" session.
- **② Strengthen / supplement holiday and weekday features**: 3 of the 5 clear misclassifications were all related to the Labor Day holiday demand; **Leisure Travel share is 68% on public holidays, 60% on weekends, and 32% on weekdays**.
- **③ Strengthen / supplement search-term semantic features**: In Necessary-purpose misclassification cases, search terms included XXX middle school, XXX building, and chain hotels such as **JI Hotel / Hanting / GreenTree Inn**; the existing feature only includes "whether the search targets a scenic-spot destination", lacking recognition of Necessary-purpose search terms.

**2. Key Takeaways**

- **The share of mixed itineraries (Necessary-purpose first, then Leisure Travel) exceeded expectations** (13% of Leisure Travel samples, 10% of Necessary-purpose samples). Splitting this same-city, continuous-demand data at the current stage is not feasible.
- **Behavioral level shows Leisure Travel characteristics while the motivation level shows Necessary-purpose — currently not resolvable**: For the two cases "burning incense at Jiuhua Mountain in Chizhou" and "returning to Beijing for an inter-city ride", the user's browsing and booking behavior **shows strong Leisure Travel characteristics**, but the underlying motivation, obtained through interviews, was Necessary-purpose.
- **2 types of noise sessions** need to be filtered out before entering the model: **① Repeated exposure from outbound ads**: a large number of `session_id`s originate from "shared or marketing campaign" sources, where the user clicks in, lands on the domestic hotel landing page, and the session ends there. **② Hotel merchants checking their own listings and competitors' listings**.
- **Leisure Travel / Necessary-purpose scenario distribution**: **Local / city leisure activities** is the second-largest Leisure Travel scenario (19% share of Leisure Travel); under the old classification logic it was categorized as Necessary-purpose.
- The fixed assumption that Necessary-purpose users stay at non-business-chain hotel types: of 6 Necessary-purpose samples, 3 stayed in 6-bed / 8-bed hostel dorm rooms.
